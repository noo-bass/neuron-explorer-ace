One plan, rendered with a push

The planner wrote one 10-second plan once. The renderer then turned that same plan into sound again and again, each time with one of its neurons (or a random direction) pushed. Anything you hear change comes from the push, because the untouched version renders identically every time.

How to read this

  • The renderer builds the sound in 8 passes, from rough noise to finished audio. A push can go into the first 4 passes, the last 4, or all 8.
  • Push size is a share of the renderer's usual internal signal at this layer, like on the planner page. These sizes are guesses for a first listen; they have not been calibrated yet.
  • Neurons were picked at random. Random rows are the yardstick: a neuron is only interesting if it does something more specific than they do.
  • "Moved" compares the raw waveforms. It is crude: a small timing shift scores high even when the music sounds the same, so trust your ears over it.
